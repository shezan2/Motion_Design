# Direction: from brief to beat sheet

## Contents
1. PAGE and the one true thing · 2. Brand inventory · 3. Three ideas of different kinds · 4. Skeletons by job · 5. Beat sheet · 6. Handoff table · 7. Copy rules · 8. Importance budget

## 1. PAGE and the one true thing
Four lines before any design:
- **Problem:** what is wrong for the viewer today, in their words.
- **Audience:** who scrolls past this, and where.
- **Goal:** the one action after watching.
- **Emotion:** what they should feel (relief, curiosity, confidence, FOMO that is real).

Then the one true thing the video is for: a specific feature, moment or offer, not "the brand". If you guessed any of it, write "Assumed:" and move on.

## 2. Brand inventory: where the idea hides
| Look at | Ask | Outtakes example |
|---|---|---|
| Logo shape | What shape lives in the mark (a square, an underscore, a curve, a dot)? | condensed italic wordmark |
| Core control | What does a user press, type or drag? | the Book button |
| Material | What is the product made of (glass UI, paper, code, photos, food)? | film strip, sports photos |
| Key verb | What does it do? | book |
| Success moment | What does "done" look like on screen? | "Booked · Sat 28 Nov", "S$70.00 paid" |
| Voice | How do they talk (dry, warm, bold, playful)? | short, confident, a bit cheeky |
| Colour logic | Ground, ink, accent; what does the accent mean? | light grey, black, neon green = action |
| Type | Their fonts; serif or sans; weights | condensed black italic + Archivo |
| Never | What would they never do? | stock photos, corporate blue |

The carried object usually comes from the first two rows. Outtakes carried the celebration photo; the Ozen rate card offers the bezier curve with handles.

## 3. Three ideas of different kinds
Write the first idea, then two more of different kinds, then pick one in a line.

| Kind | What it looks like | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| UI documentary of one task | follow one person through one job, start to done | Outtakes, Zapier |
| Chaos to order | the problem as noise, the product as convergence into the brand | Zapier, OpenAI |
| Typographic essay | copy-led kinetic type with icons as metaphors | divyansh.fx |
| Transformation chain | one brand shape morphs through every scene | youmotion |
| Real-output montage | many real results, fast (designs, dishes, listings, photos) | OpenAI poster burst |
| Constellation and zoom | product pieces orbit the logo, then each gets a close-up | Apple Widgets |
| Search and find | the viewer searches, finds the brand, explores | calbby.mg |
| Before and after | static vs moving, manual vs automated, messy vs sorted | (Ozen's own ad) |
| The brand's gesture | a swipe, stamp, pour or tap becomes the rhythm of the film | (any brand with a signature action) |

Tests before picking:
- **Cover the logo:** if a competitor's logo would fit, the idea is generic.
- **Thumbnail:** would the hero frame stop a thumb mid-scroll?
- **First 3 seconds:** do they say what this is, or open a question worth staying for?

## 4. Skeletons by job
Starting timings in seconds for 9:16. Bend them to the idea; never pad to fill a slot.

### 4.1 SaaS or app explainer
| Beat | Role | 15 s | 30 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The pain as noise or a sharp question | hook | 0-2.5 | 0-4 | 0-6 |
| The product arrives (the noise converges) | turn | 2.5-4.5 | 4-7 | 6-11 |
| The core action, performed in real UI | proof | 4.5-8 | 7-13 | 11-22 |
| Second (and third) proof | proof | 8-11 | 13-21 | 22-44 |
| Payoff line | point | 11-12.5 | 21-24 | 44-50 |
| Brand and call to action | cta | 12.5-15 | 24-30 | 50-60 |

### 4.2 Product or feature launch ("Introducing")
Tease a detail (0-3 s) -> reveal the name and hero shot -> 2-3 features as quick proofs -> availability (real date, price, where) -> call to action. Apple Widgets is the model.

### 4.3 Local service business (salon, clinic, F&B, property, tuition, trades)
The customer's moment or problem -> the offer (what they get, the price if public) -> proof (the client's own photos or footage; reviews only if real and approved) -> how to book (WhatsApp, booking link, location) -> logo. Small businesses rarely have product UI: their photos, menu, price list or WhatsApp chat become the "UI", with kinetic type over real images and a chat bubble as the call to action.

### 4.4 Brand manifesto, copy-led
Provocation -> tension in 2-3 lines -> reframe -> what the brand believes -> logo. Lives or dies on the script (divyansh.fx).

### 4.5 Logo sting or opener (3-8 s)
The carrier enters -> transforms once or twice -> the logo resolves -> holds 0.6-1 s (youmotion).

### 4.6 Event or promo announcement
What, when, where, why now (only a real reason) -> one big date treatment -> call to action.

### 4.7 Hype teaser
Fast montage under one treatment, a persistent small caption, a date or "coming soon", a small logo (BLACKBOX.AI). Footage must be the client's or licensed.

### 4.8 Ozen's own portfolio post
The client film inside a card with a chapter bar (Ava AI frame), or a before/after split of the static brief and the finished motion.

## 5. Beat sheet
One row per beat, filled before building:

| t (s) | role | importance | buys: the viewer... | copy (screen / VO) | picture with the text covered | in -> out | handoff / carrier | sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0-2.4 | hook | 2 | feels their DMs are chaos | "Still answering every DM?" | a phone overflowing with message bubbles | bubbles drop 2 f apart -> whip up | the last bubble becomes the search bar | soft pops, whoosh |

- **Buys:** finish "the viewer now believes ___". If you can't, cut the beat.
- **Picture with the text covered:** if the frame says nothing without its words, it's a slide.
- Check the total against the plan's cap, including reading holds and the end hold.

## 6. Handoff table
One row per seam, written before animating either side:

| Seam | A's last frame: object, position, scale, colour, blur, velocity | B's first frame: the same fields | Carrier | Cut type | Frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

If A's exit and B's entry don't match (axis, direction, speed, both mid-motion), fix the plan, not the easing.

## 7. Copy rules
- 2-6 words per on-screen beat; a voiceover can carry more.
- Use the client's voice and real phrasing (site, reviews, the brief), then cut half.
- Specific beats generic: "Booked at your rate" beats "Unlock your potential".
- Avoid unless it is truly the client's voice: "Unlock", "Elevate", "Meet X", "the future of", "game-changer", "seamless", triplets of one-word sentences, "Not just X. It's Y."
- Facts exactly as given: prices as S$70, dates as "24 Nov" or "24 November", product names spelled the client's way, URLs in lowercase.
- Calls to action say what to do and where: "DM 'VIDEO'", "Book at outtakes.sg", "Link in bio".
- Write plain English for a Singapore audience; no forced slang.
- Any copy longer than a line (captions, scripts, posts) gets a humanizer pass: no em-dash chains, no "it's not X, it's Y", no stacked threes, no inflated words. Shezan has rejected copy that "still sounds AI written".

## 8. Importance budget
Give every beat a number: 1 connective (fast and small, a cut or a word swap), 2 supporting, 3 the point (larger, slower, held for reading time plus 0.5-1 s, with its own sound). At most a quarter of beats are a 3. Emphasise with scale first, then colour, then isolation; weight comes last. Animating every line the same way is the most common AI tell.

# Outtakes blog: SEO and traffic plan

## What the launch post now does

- It targets one search: "sports photographer Singapore", with football variants ("football photographer Singapore", "hire a photographer for a football match"). These are service-plus-location phrases, which people type when they're ready to book.
- The keyword is in the SEO title (50 characters), the H1, the URL slug, the first paragraph and one H2. It isn't repeated beyond that, because stuffing hurts more than it helps.
- The first paragraph answers the search on its own: what Outtakes is, where, when, and what you can do. Google's AI Overviews and snippets tend to quote this kind of self-contained opening.
- "Questions people ask" is written as visible page copy. Google stopped showing FAQ rich results in May 2026, so the questions are there for readers and AI answers, not for a special search display.
- `BlogPosting` structured data (JSON-LD) is at the bottom of the file, ready to paste into the page head.
- Two calls to action link into the product ("Find a photographer", "Join as a photographer"). Point them at the real search and sign-up pages once you know those URLs.
- Image alt text is written for the two suggested images. Use real Outtakes photos, compressed to under about 200 KB each.

## Before you publish

1. Set the slug to `/blog/book-sports-photographer-singapore` and keep it forever. Changing URLs later loses ranking.
2. Make a 1200×630 share image (`og_image`) so the link looks good on WhatsApp, Instagram stories and LinkedIn.
3. Add the site to Google Search Console, submit the sitemap, and request indexing for the post on launch day.
4. Set up a Google Business Profile for Outtakes, if you're eligible, with the website link. It helps "near me" and Singapore searches.
5. Check the page on a phone: it should load fast, with no layout jumps while images load.

## Getting traffic to it

Search takes weeks to pick up a new site, so the first traffic will come from your own channels:

- Link the post from the launch Reel caption, your bio link and the story on 20 Nov.
- Ask every photographer on the platform to link to Outtakes from their own Instagram bio and website. Links from real Singapore sports accounts are the strongest signal a new site can get.
- Ask the clubs and leagues you already shoot for (school teams, amateur leagues, futsal groups) to link to their photographers' Outtakes pages.
- Post a short version on LinkedIn for brands and companies, who are the clients that need invoices.

## Posts to write next (one every week or two)

Each one targets a search that clients or photographers actually make, and links back to the launch post and to photographer profiles.

| Post | Search it targets | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| How much does a sports photographer cost in Singapore? | sports photographer price Singapore | clients |
| How to book a photographer for your football match | football match photographer Singapore | clients |
| Shot of the Week: the story behind the photo (weekly) | brand and photographer names | everyone, and it gets shared |
| Best spots to shoot football in Singapore at night | football photography Singapore | photographers |
| What to put in a sports photography invoice | sports photography invoice template | photographers |
| How to price your sports photography as a student | sports photography rates | photographers |
| Photographer spotlight: [name] | the photographer's name | everyone |

Rules for these:
- Write from experience: real matches, real venues, real numbers you can stand behind. Google rates first-hand content higher than generic articles.
- One clear search per post. Don't write two posts aiming at the same search.
- Update older posts when facts change, and change `dateModified` when you do.

## What to measure

- **In Search Console:** impressions and clicks for "sports photographer Singapore" and the football variants. Expect very little in the first month, then steady growth.
- **In analytics:** visits to the blog that go on to a profile or to sign-up. That number matters more than raw traffic.
